The Caribbean

Sheet 11

Jamaica

Great Antilles · independent Commonwealth member from 1962

Conquered by the English in 1655, Jamaica became an independent Commonwealth member on 6 August 1962. A hand-painted map of the Caribbean basin anchors this sheet, with a 1937 Coronation pair, a UPU 75th-anniversary issue and four 1962 Independence commemoratives — the Doctor Bird, Blue Mountain Peak, the national coat of arms on a £1 value, and the flag.

Sheet 12

Cayman Islands

Three islands, British colony · capital Georgetown

Jamaica's stamps served the Cayman Islands from 1889 until 1901, when the colony began issuing its own. Five stamps are mounted here, from an Edward VII portrait through a George VI sailing-ship pictorial to a QEII outline-map definitive.

Sheet 13

British Honduras / Belize

Crown colony to 1973 · independent Belize from 1981

British Honduras issued stamps under that name from 1866 to 1973, when the territory was renamed Belize; full independence followed on 21 September 1981. This page carries eleven early Queen Victoria and Edward VII definitives on the main sheet, plus a Stanley Airport pictorial and a butterfly-and-QEII design on the supplement.

Sheet 14

Dominican Republic — British Post Offices

British postal agencies at Porto Plata and Santo Domingo

British post offices once operated in Dominican ports, using distinctive numbered barred cancels — Porto Plata (1871–1879, No. C 86) and Santo Domingo (1865–1879, No. C 87). No British-era covers survive here, only two later national Dominican issues: an anti-malaria stamp and an education-centenary commemorative.

Sheet 15

Leeward Islands, British Virgin Islands & Barbuda

A shared crown colony of small Antillean territories

The Leeward Islands crown colony grouped the Virgin Islands, Barbuda, Antigua, Anguilla, St Christopher-Nevis, Montserrat and Dominica under one general stamp issue from 1890, withdrawn in 1956; individual islands also kept their own issues. The British Virgin Islands printed stamps from 1866 — four are shown, including an early Half Penny and a QEII map-of-Tortola design.

Sheet 16

Antigua & Barbuda

Leeward Islands presidency to associated statehood, 1967

Antigua was a presidency of the Leeward Islands crown colony until 1956, an associated state from 1967, and independent from Britain together with Barbuda on 1 November 1981. Eleven stamps run from Victorian issues in orange-red, through a St John's Harbour George VI pictorial, to a Postage Stamp Centenary pair marking 1862–1962.

Sheet 17

Anguilla

Broke from St Kitts-Nevis in 1967

Part of the joint postal territory of St Christopher-Nevis-Anguilla from 1952, Anguilla broke relations with the other two islands on 30 May 1967 and began issuing its own stamps that year, gaining a separate government on 10 February 1976. No stamps of Anguilla's own survive mounted on this page — only the historical note beside Antigua's stamps.

Sheet 18

St Christopher, Nevis & St Kitts-Nevis-Anguilla

A shared postal history across three linked island names

St Christopher issued its own stamps from 1870, sharing issues with the Leeward Islands from 1890 and with Nevis from 1903; Nevis had its own stamps from 1861. United as the crown colony St Kitts-Nevis in 1956, the joint territory used combined "Christopher-Nevis-Anguilla" stamps from 1952–1980. Five QEII pictorials are mounted here — Salt Pond, Bath House and coastal-view designs.

Sheet 19

Montserrat

Leeward Islands · autonomous from 1963

A former British crown colony among the Leeward Islands, Montserrat gained autonomy in 1963. Five stamps are shown here, from George V portrait definitives through a 1937 Royal Wedding issue to a modern Churchill/QEII joint commemorative alongside a Machin definitive.

Sheet 20

Dominica

Discovered by Columbus on a Sunday, 1493 · hence the name

Long disputed between France and England, Dominica was taken by the English in 1759 and became an associated state in 1967, independent from 3 November 1978. Four stamps run from an early one-penny "War Tax" overprint to a vanilla-drying and river pictorial pair in the QEII series.
