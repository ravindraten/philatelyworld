Rubik's Cube at 50: A Stamp Set You Can Fold into a Cube

Magyar Posta marks half a century since Ernő Rubik filed the Cube's patent with a numbered souvenir sheet, a foldable cube stamp set and a Retro Cube first day cover

Published by: Philately World Category: Innovative Stamps · Puzzle Stamps · Hungary 2025 Series: 50 Years of the Rubik's Cube® Country: Hungary

On 30 January 2025, Magyar Posta celebrated the 50th anniversary of the patent filing of the Rubik's Cube® with a philatelic tribute unlike any other. The issue centres on a numbered souvenir sheet showing a scrambled Cube, and an exclusive stamp set that can itself be folded into a cube. The souvenir sheet and the related products were designed by the graphic artist Attila André Elekes.

The issued souvenir sheet shows a scrambled Rubik's Cube®, framed by squares and rectangles in the colours of the Cube. Image: collector photograph.

From Magic Cube to a Global Icon

The Rubik's Cube® — originally called the Magic Cube — was invented by the Hungarian architect, interior designer, designer, toy designer and sculptor Ernő Rubik Jr. A Kossuth and State Prize winner, honorary professor, Artist of the Nation and member of the European Academy of Sciences, Rubik created many logic toys, but his most famous invention became a worldwide phenomenon.

He applied for a patent for the Cube on 30 January 1975, and the patent was granted on 31 December 1977. Success followed quickly. More than a simple game, the Cube draws on concentration, curiosity, playfulness and the desire to find a solution. It has appeared in books, plays and Hollywood films, has been taught in university courses, and is used as sporting equipment at world speedsolving competitions. In almost half a century it has become the game of a billion people.

The Souvenir Sheet

The souvenir sheet places a scrambled Rubik's Cube® at its centre, while the squares and rectangles that form the frame take the colours of the Cube. The Hungarian version of the official anniversary logo — “50 Years of the Rubik's Cube®”, or 50 éves a Rubik kocka — appears on the sheet, on the first day cover and in the imprint of the postmark.

The sheet measures 80 × 80 mm imperforate, and the stamp has a circular perforation with a diameter of 37 mm — a playful break from the usual rectangular stamp shape. It is printed by ANY Security Printing Company using four-colour offset on white, wood-free, surface-treated gummed stamp paper of 110 g/m² with an optical whitener.

Forty thousand souvenir sheets were produced.

A Stamp Set That Becomes a Cube

The real showpiece for collectors is the exclusive stamp set, which turns postage stamps into a three-dimensional object. In the standard edition, the Rubik's Cube® stamp set can be assembled after purchase; the cube-shaped edition arrives already assembled. The Cube and its accompanying holder can also be displayed as a decorative object.

The standard edition of the stamp set can be folded into a cube after purchase. Image: Magyar Posta.

The standard edition, sold in a resealable case, was produced in 2,000 copies, while the plastic box edition was limited to 1,000 copies. The sheets in the Cube set are numbered 1 to 1,000, and those in the standard edition 1,001 to 3,000. The cube version could only be ordered and purchased at specialised postal services and at Filaposta.

Order codes are 2025021030011 (standard edition) and 2025022030011 (Cube set).

The Retro Cube First Day Cover

The special first day cover features an image of the so-called Retro Rubik's Cube®, and the anniversary logo appears in the postmark imprint. The FDC was printed by Codex Zrt., while the souvenir sheet and stamp set were printed by ANY Security Printing Company.

Collector Appeal

This issue sits at the intersection of several popular themes: anniversary stamps, Hungarian design, toy and puzzle topicals, and unusual formats. A stamp set that folds into a cube — and a numbered souvenir sheet with a circular perforation — makes it instantly recognisable in any collection.

The very small editions of the Cube set (1,000) and standard set (2,000) add real scarcity, while the 40,000-sheet print run keeps the individual souvenir sheet obtainable for most collectors. For anyone who collects innovative philately or grew up with a Rubik's Cube in hand, this release is a natural centrepiece.
