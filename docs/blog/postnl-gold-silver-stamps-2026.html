Postzegel met Goud – Delfts Blauw, one of the highlights of PostNL's 2026 gold and silver programme

Every year, PostNL's Collect Club adds a small number of gold and silver stamps to its programme. These are not ordinary postage stamps: the gold issues are made with 24-carat gold, the silver issues contain silver, and both are produced in limited editions with special presentation. In 2026 the programme spanned art, comics, a children's icon, and a four-part tribute to Dutch ships. This guide brings the year's issues together in one place.

The 2026 Gold Stamps

PostNL's gold stamps are made with 24-carat gold and are strictly limited. They tend to celebrate subjects with a strong visual identity — a painting, a character, a piece of Dutch heritage — and are presented as collector's items rather than everyday postage. The 2026 gold programme brought three very different subjects to the same precious-metal format.

Het Puttertje (The Goldfinch)

Het Puttertje, Carel Fabritius's 1654 painting on a 24-carat gold stamp

Het Puttertje brings Carel Fabritius's 1654 masterpiece to a gold stamp. The painting, one of the treasures of the Mauritshuis in The Hague, is famous for its simplicity and for the extraordinary technique with which Fabritius painted the bird's feathers and its shadow on the wall. On gold, that texture takes on a new character. The issue coincided with the Mauritshuis exhibition BIRDS, curated by The Goldfinch and Simon Schama. Read more about the Het Puttertje gold stamp.

Garfield

Garfield in 24-carat gold, issued on 27 April 2026

Garfield is the lighter counterpart: the lasagne-loving comic cat, immortalised in gold on 27 April 2026 in an edition of 1,000 copies. The design uses a standing portrait of Garfield, developed specifically for the gold issue and deliberately different from his appearance on the regular frame stamps. Delfts Blauw in 24-carat gold, a collaboration with Royal Delft

The 2026 Silver Stamps

The silver issues of 2026 followed two themes: the Dutch merchant and naval fleet, and the world of nijntje. Silver stamps from PostNL contain silver and are produced in limited editions, often with special packaging.

Dutch Ships: Zwarte Zee

Zwarte Zee, the 1933 Smit tug, on the first stamp of the Dutch Ships series

The Nederlandse Schepen (Dutch Ships) series ran across the year in four parts, each dedicated to a Dutch ship built in the twentieth century. The first was the Zwarte Zee, the 1933 Smit Internationale tug built in Kinderdijk. She was launched on 2 June 1933 and made her first official trial trip on 3 October that year with Princess Juliana on board. Smit was taken over by Boskalis in 2010.

Dutch Ships: Willem Ruys

Willem Ruys, the Rotterdamsche Lloyd passenger ship, on silver

Willem Ruys was ordered in 1938 by De Rotterdamsche Lloyd from the Koninklijke Maatschappij De Schelde in Vlissingen. The Second World War prevented her completion, and the hull stood on the slipway in central Vlissingen for seven and a half years. When the Walcheren dykes were bombed in 1944 and much of the island flooded, her sloops were used for transport over water.

Dutch Ships: Oranje Nassau

Oranje Nassau, built in 1957 for the KNSM, on the third silver stamp

Oranje Nassau was built in 1957 for the Koninklijke Nederlandse Stoomboot Maatschappij (KNSM) at Scheepswerven Gebroeders Pot in Bolnes, near Rotterdam. She was designed to carry both cargo and passengers, and is remembered as one of the last classic Dutch combined passenger-and-cargo ships linking the Netherlands with the former colonies in the Caribbean.

Dutch Ships: Noord Hinder

Noord Hinder, the last Dutch lightship, closes the four-part series

Noord Hinder closes the series. The 45-metre lightship held a fixed position west of Walcheren near the Noord-Hinder sandbank. Besides acting as a lighthouse, she carried instruments for measuring water and air temperature, wave height, wind speed and cloud cover, and supplied daily observations to the Royal Netherlands Meteorological Institute. On 21 March 1994 she was taken out of service as the last Dutch lightship and moved to Hellevoetsluis, where she is open to the public as a museum ship. nijntje en nina op vakantie

nijntje en nina op vakantie, PostNL's first stamp sheet with two silver stamps

nijntje en nina op vakantie, issued on 12 August 2026, was a milestone: the first PostNL stamp sheet to contain two silver stamps. Before this issue, PostNL had only produced single stamps with silver. The sheet comes in a luxury presentation with a certificate of authenticity, and its limited edition makes it one of the most sought-after Dutch issues of the year. The drawings were chosen by Mariëlla Ostendorf of Mercis, which manages Dick Bruna's work, and the stamps use matte silver with glossy silver accents. Read more about the nijntje silver stamp sheet.

How to Collect Them

Gold and silver stamps are usually kept in their original presentation rather than used on mail. Their value lies in the combination of a limited edition, precious metal, and a subject that matters to collectors. For a focused collection, the Dutch Ships series works well as a complete set of four, while the gold issues can be grouped by theme: Dutch art (Het Puttertje, Delfts Blauw) or popular culture (Garfield). The nijntje sheet stands on its own as a milestone in PostNL's use of silver.

Why This Issue Matters

PostNL's gold and silver stamps are where philately meets craft: precious metal, limited editions, and designs made to be kept rather than spent. The 2026 programme shows the range of that idea, from a seventeenth-century masterpiece and a Dutch design icon to a comic-strip cat and a four-part maritime series. Together they form a compact record of what Dutch stamp design can do when it works at the intersection of art, history and collecting.
