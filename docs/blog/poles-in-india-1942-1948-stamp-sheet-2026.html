Poles in India 1942–1948: A Stamp Tribute to Polish Refugees in India

Poczta Polska commemorates the thousands of Polish refugees who found shelter and friendship in India during the Second World War

← Back to Philately World
Published by: Philately World Category: Poland 2026 · Commemorative Stamps Series: Poles in India 1942–1948 Country: Poland

On 22 October 2026, Poczta Polska issued the philatelic series “Poles in India 1942–1948”. The issue commemorates an extraordinary chapter in the shared history of Poland and India and recalls the fate of thousands of Polish refugees who found shelter in India during the Second World War. After experiencing war, deportation and forced displacement, many Polish citizens — including numerous families, children and scouts — found safety, kindness and support on Indian soil.

Thanks to the assistance provided by Indian authorities and local communities, Polish refugees were able to rebuild their daily lives, continue education, maintain social organisations and scouting activities, and preserve their ties with their homeland. The issue is both a tribute to the help that was offered and a reminder of the unique friendship that developed between the Polish and Indian peoples during a difficult period of history.

The 20-stamp commemorative sheet, arranged in a 5 × 4 layout with a repeated motif celebrating Polish–Indian friendship.

The Design

The stamp features a symbolic composition referring to the history of Polish refugees in India between 1942 and 1948 and to the friendship between Poland and India. The central motif presents two figures wearing traditional folk costumes: a figure inspired by Polish folk culture and a figure inspired by Indian cultural traditions. Their shared dance symbolises cultural dialogue, mutual respect and the bond between two nations connected through common historical experiences.

On the left side of the design appears the Scout Fleur-de-Lis, commemorating the presence of Polish scouts among the refugees living in India. On the right side is a lotus flower, one of the most important symbols of Indian culture, associated with purity, spirituality and renewal.

The design was prepared in consultation with, and approved by, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the Republic of Poland — a diplomatic as well as a philatelic tribute.

Architectural Symbols and Colours

The background of each stamp incorporates two important architectural symbols. The Wawel Royal Castle in Kraków is one of the most important symbols of Polish history, statehood and national heritage; its presence recalls the homeland to which Polish refugees remained deeply attached despite years spent abroad. The Red Fort in New Delhi is one of the most recognisable monuments of India and an important symbol of the country's history and culture; its inclusion highlights India's role as a place of refuge and assistance for Polish citizens during the Second World War.

Colour also plays an important role in the design. The left side is dominated by shades of red and white referring to the national colours of Poland and symbolising remembrance of the homeland. The right side incorporates orange, green and gold — colours associated with Indian culture, traditions and artistic heritage. Above the entire composition rises a radiant sun whose rays illuminate both sides of the design, a motif symbolising hope, safety, solidarity, friendship between nations and the humanitarian support extended to the Polish people.

The 20-Stamp Sheet

The sheet consists of 20 postage stamps arranged in a 5 × 4 layout. The repeated motif creates a colourful visual narrative about the meeting of two cultures and the values of friendship, solidarity and humanitarian assistance.

The First Day Cover

The First Day Cover features a historical photograph showing Polish refugees living in India between 1942 and 1948. In the foreground are young girls dressed in traditional Polish folk costumes, while members of the local Indian community can be seen in the background. The photograph comes from the collections of the KARTA Center Foundation and provides a valuable visual testimony to everyday life within the Polish refugee community in India.

Collector Appeal

This issue will appeal to collectors of Polish philately, World War II and refugee history, and India-related thematics. The combination of a deeply human story, rich symbolism and a full set of collecting formats — single stamp, cancelled stamp, First Day Cover and 20-stamp sheet — makes it a meaningful addition to any collection.

The design's pairing of Wawel Royal Castle and the Red Fort, together with the Polish and Indian folk figures and the Scout Fleur-de-Lis, gives the issue strong thematic depth for topical collectors as well as for anyone interested in the enduring friendship between Poland and India.
