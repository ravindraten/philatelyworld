Coffee Bean: The World's First Coffee-Scented Coffee Leather Stamp

Austrian Post pushes philatelic innovation further with a miniature sheet made from real recycled coffee grounds — shaped like a coffee bean and naturally smelling of coffee

← Back to Philately World
Published by: Philately World Category: Innovative Stamps · Austria 2026 Series: Coffee Bean Country: Austria

On 1 October 2026, Austrian Post will issue what may be the world's most aromatic postage stamp: the Coffee Bean miniature sheet — a stamp made from actual coffee leather crafted from recycled coffee grounds. Designed by Anja Derkits and produced using DTF transfer printing, this issue continues Austria's tradition of groundbreaking philatelic innovation (following the 2025 3D-printed Edelweiss stamp and the embroidered Petit Point rose stamp of 2010).

The stamp is not merely coffee-themed — it is coffee. The substrate is a vegan "coffee leather" created by sieving fine, dried coffee grounds onto a natural fleece, spraying with an ecological binding agent, and pressing into a thin, flexible, tear-resistant sheet through multiple stages. The material retains the slightly grainy texture of ground coffee and, remarkably, naturally smells of coffee. After printing, an adhesive film was applied and the stamp die-cut in the shape of a coffee bean, complete with the characteristic central groove.

From Waste to Wonder: The Upcycling Story

The coffee grounds used in this issue were collected from offices and hospitals — a waste stream that would otherwise be discarded. Austrian Post has transformed this into a philatelic product that engages multiple senses: sight (the rich brown colour and bean shape), touch (the grainy, leather-like texture), and smell (the unmistakable aroma of coffee). It is a tangible demonstration of circular economy principles applied to stamp production.

As the official description notes: "Coffee is indispensable for many people whether drunk early in the morning to wake them up or as a reviving aromatic drink throughout the day. But once the coffee has been drunk, we are left with the coffee grounds, a valuable material that it is a shame to just throw away."

Austrian Post: A History of Philatelic Firsts

This issue fits squarely in Austrian Post's innovative lineage. In 2010, they issued the Petit Point embroidered rose stamp — a miniature sheet with a real hand-embroidered flower at its centre. In 2025, they released the world's first 3D-printed stamp (the €6.50 Edelweiss by Hammerle & Vogel). Now in 2026, they add coffee leather to the roster of unconventional stamp substrates — joining embroidery, 3D printing, and now upcycled organic waste.

The Coffee Bean stamp is a miniature sheet measuring 33 × 43 mm with a face value of €6.70. It is a genuine postage stamp — fully valid for mailing — that happens to be made from recycled coffee grounds and smell like your morning espresso.

Why This Matters for Collectors

The Coffee Bean stamp represents a new frontier in material innovation in philately. For thematic collectors, it fits "beverages," "food," "innovation," "upcycling/sustainability," and "unusual materials." For specialists of Austrian philately, it continues the prestigious series of experimental issues. For the broader collecting community, it is a conversation piece — a stamp you can genuinely smell.

The use of DTF transfer printing on a textured, fibrous organic substrate is a technical achievement in itself. The coffee leather's flexibility and tear resistance had to be balanced with print adhesion and gumming — a challenge solved by the post-printing adhesive film application.
