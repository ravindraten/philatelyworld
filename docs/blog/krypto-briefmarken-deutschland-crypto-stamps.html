Krypto-Briefmarken: Real Stamps + NFT

From the AI-pixelated Brandenburger Tor to the Marianas Trench — Deutsche Post's "Historische Bauwerke in Deutschland" and "Der Mensch als Entdecker" phygital stamp series, a genuine self-adhesive stamp with its NFT twin on the blockchain

The Deutsche Post Krypto-Briefmarke (crypto stamp) is one of the most talked-about innovations in German philately since 2023. Each Krypto-Briefmarke is a phygital collector's item: a real, self-adhesive German special stamp value (SPWZ) combined with a digital twin — an NFT whose authenticity is guaranteed by blockchain technology. Since the programme began, every edition has followed the same clever recipe, and two thematically rich series have grown out of it.

The launch of "Historische Bauwerke in Deutschland" (Historical Buildings in Germany) in November 2023 made Deutsche Post the first postal operator to combine a real stamp with an AI-generated motif and a blockchain twin. The series ran for five editions — from the Brandenburg Gate to the Semperoper — before a brand-new series, "Der Mensch als Entdecker" (Man as Explorer), opened in June 2026 with the Marianas Trench. Here is the whole programme, issue by issue.

One Edition, Two Worlds: Real Stamp + NFT

A Krypto-Briefmarke always combines a genuine self-adhesive German stamp with its digital twin — a digital image linked to an NFT whose authenticity is guaranteed by blockchain technology. With the purchase you receive the access information that allows you to become the owner of a unique, digital collector's item on a Ciphers.me account — Deutsche Post's cooperative partner platform and an innovation of the Dutch security printer Royal Joh. Enschedé, whereby the NFT is minted on the Polygon blockchain. This way you can present and collect your crypto stamp digitally as well.

Since the Kölner Dom edition, each issue has appeared in four colour variants with different rarity levels. The self-adhesive stamp itself is always identical; only the colouring of the product design — and the matching NFT — varies. A pink product, for example, goes with a pink NFT. Choosing a colour at order time is not possible: the crypto stamp is dispatched at random in a neutral, sealed envelope. Once the envelope has been opened, the statutory right of withdrawal lapses and returns are excluded (maximum order: 10 pieces per edition).

Series One: Historische Bauwerke in Deutschland (2023–2025)

The first series took German landmarks into the digital age. Every motif was interpreted by artificial intelligence — never a photorealistic copy, but the AI's own vision of the building, with deviations deliberately accepted and not "corrected" by human hands. Each of the five editions also appeared as a classic, water-activated stamp without NFT; the crypto versions added the phygital layer.

Series Two: Der Mensch als Entdecker (from 2026)

With the Marianengraben (Marianas Trench), Deutsche Post launched a brand-new series in June 2026: "Der Mensch als Entdecker" (Man as Explorer). It presents outstanding events that expanded — and in some cases permanently changed — our understanding of the world and the cosmos. The subscription seems to be a basic need of our species: setting off for foreign shores, climbing the highest mountains, descending into the deepest depths of the earth and pushing the boundaries of knowledge into unknown territory.

The Marianengraben crypto stamp (€1.80 SPWZ, issued 11 June 2026) was limited to 50,000 copies and introduced a new colour scheme with its own rarity stages — pink 5%, blue 10%, green 35% and orange 50%. As the very first cryptographic edition, the motif, a submarine whose spotlight scans the rocks of the trench, was interpreted by the AI DALL-E. A second series issue, themed around the Himalaya, is already announced for 1 October 2026.

The Album: Historische Bauwerke in Deutschland

Completing the programme is the official showcase album (art no. 150402025, €39.90): a high-quality, foil-finished hardcover album in the classic format 21 × 21 cm, with unstocked insert pockets for all five crypto stamp editions of the "Historische Bauwerke in Deutschland" series issued so far. As exclusive bonus material, the album holds the bonus NFT "Reichstagsgebäude" and a hologram of the 1986 Reichstagsgebäude Berlin stamp, together with all five special stamps of the series (Bund 2023–2025, mint in protective mounts).

The bonus NFT rounds off the architecture series with the most moving German building of all: the Reichstag in Berlin. Built 1884–1894 in Neo-Renaissance style to the plans of Paul Wallot, it saw Philipp Scheidemann proclaim the Republic and the end of the monarchy from one of its balconies in 1918. The Reichstag fire of 1933 then served the National Socialists as a pretext for dismantling the rule of law. After the reunification, the Reichstag returned to the heart of German democracy with its iconic glass cupola, completed in 1999.

Why This Programme Matters

The Krypto-Briefmarken are a historic first for German philately: Deutsche Post combined a real, franking-valid self-adhesive stamp with a blockchain twin, and did so through AI-generated design. The combined appeal of heritage architecture, algorithmic art and the colour-rarity game has turned every edition into a small lottery of collectability — a pink Dom or a blue Marianas Trench leaves the printer as an instantly recognisable rarity tier.

For collectors of German special stamps, AI art, phygital or crypto philately — or simply of five unforgettable German landmarks — the programme offers a vivid bridge from classic stamp collecting into the digital age. With the new "Der Mensch als Entdecker" series, the story is only just beginning.
