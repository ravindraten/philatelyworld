Truth and Reconciliation: Canada's T-Shirt Shaped Orange Shirt Stamps

Four self-adhesive stamps die-cut in the shape of T-shirts, honouring Survivors, First Nations, Inuit and Métis communities, and Mother Earth

On 29 September 2026, Canada Post issued a poignant set of four stamps commemorating Canada's journey toward truth and reconciliation. In a striking act of philatelic design, the stamps are die-cut in the shape of T-shirts — each one an orange shirt carrying a message of remembrance, healing and hope.

The four orange shirt stamps, die-cut in a playful T-shirt silhouette.

Why Orange Shirts?

The stamps honour Survivors and their families, as well as the children who never made it home from the residential school system — a legacy of trauma and abuse inflicted on Indigenous Peoples in Canada. The orange shirt has become the defining symbol of this remembrance: Orange Shirt Day began in 2013 as a grassroots initiative to recognize and honour the Indigenous children who were taken from their families and placed in residential schools.

"Every child matters." The orange shirt symbolizes the removal of Indigenous children from their homes, the loss of their culture and freedom, and the hope for reconciliation and healing.

The Four Designs

The set tells a story across four shirts. Three stamps reflect the experiences of the First Nations, Inuit and Métis communities, while the fourth stamp is dedicated to Mother Earth, symbolizing the connection between past, present and future generations.

The four individual stamps — three honouring First Nations, Inuit and Métis experiences, and one dedicated to Mother Earth.

Booklet Format

The four stamps were issued in a booklet of 8 self-adhesive Permanent stamps — each design appearing twice. As Permanent denominations, they are always valid for the current domestic postage rate, making them as practical as they are meaningful.

Why This Issue Stands Out

Beyond their powerful message, these stamps are a wonderful example of odd shaped philately. The T-shirt die-cut turns each stamp into a miniature garment — instantly recognizable, playful in form, yet deeply serious in meaning. Issues like this bridge the gap between thematic collecting and social history, and they are always favourites among collectors of unusual stamp shapes and Canada thematic albums alike.
