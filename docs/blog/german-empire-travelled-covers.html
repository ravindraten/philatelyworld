Travelled Covers of the Deutsches Reich

Sixteen album leaves of franked, postmarked envelopes — from Reichspost carmine numerals to Hindenburg definitives — read stamp by stamp, cover by cover.

This album gathers commercially travelled covers — real posted mail, not mint stamps in a stockbook — carried across five and a half decades of German postal history. The run opens in the Reichspost years of the 1880s–90s, moves through the long-running Germania definitive series that carried German mail from 1900 to the early 1920s, passes through the inflation overprints of 1922–23, and closes with Weimar and early Third Reich issues, including Hindenburg definitives and swastika slogan cancels of the late 1930s.

A striking number of the envelopes are addressed to members of the Ziegler family of Hersfeld (Hesse), giving the collection the flavour of a single family's correspondence archive rather than a random assortment — postmarked at small-town offices like Hersfeld, Cassel, Dornberg and Marburg alongside city offices in Dresden, Frankfurt and Berlin.

Below, each album leaf is shown as scanned, with a short read on every stamp and cancellation visible on its covers.

Leaf 01

Germania Reds and Greens

Four covers, Weimar Hindenburg red to early Germania pairs.
  • 12pf Hindenburg definitive

    A red 12-pfennig portrait stamp addressed to Hersfeld, part of the later Weimar-era definitive series. The bar cancel obscures most of the town name but the "Deutsches Reich" inscription is clear.

  • Pair of 5pf green Germania

    Two 5-pfennig Germania stamps tied by a Pirna cancel dated 5.3.04. A standard domestic letter rate made up from two low values rather than one stamp.

  • Reichspost ship-design stamp

    An olive-brown stamp on the reverse of a cover, part-struck by a town cancel ending "…-berg". The design shows the Reichspost's steamship motif used on the higher denominations.

  • 10pf red Germania

    A single 10-pfennig Germania paying a standard letter, cancelled at Mainz. Addressed to a "Fräulein" at Laubenheim on the Rhine.

Leaf 02

Numerals, Reds and the Hindenburg Browns

Five covers spanning Germania numerals to Hindenburg definitives.
  • 2pf black Germania numeral

    A low-value black numeral stamp to Marburg, marked "frei" (postage paid/franked) by the sender. Typical of a printed-matter or short-distance local rate.

  • Brown Germania definitive

    Cancelled at Hersfeld in 1921, addressed within the same postal district. A routine domestic letter of the early Weimar period.

  • 10pf red Germania

    Franks a letter to Professor Dr. Karl Reuschel in Wiesbaden, cancelled at Dresden in 1910. A well-struck circular date stamp confirms the year.

  • 3pf brown Hindenburg definitive

    Portrait definitive paying a printed-matter or postcard-equivalent rate. Cancellation is only partially legible.

  • 10pf red Germania

    Cancelled "Doernberg" in 1900, one of the earliest-dated Germania strikes in the album. Addressed to Herr Lehrer P. Ziegler in Hersfeld.

Leaf 03

Hindenburg Definitives and Wartime Post

Feldpost, a 1936 letter, and a 1940 slogan-cancelled cover.
  • 15pf red Hindenburg definitive

    A Feldpostbrief (military field-post letter) sent in 1927 from Etzel, franked despite the "Feldpost" designation which normally travelled free. Addressed to Winkel.

  • 12pf red Hindenburg definitive

    Cancelled at Ruppersdorf in March 1936, sent to Fräulein Ilse Böttger in Sebnitz. A clean strike shows the full circular datestamp.

  • Trio of 1pf black Hindenburg coils

    Two plain 1-pfennig coil stamps plus a third bearing a printed "0,40" control tab, made up to pay a parcel or bulk rate. Cancelled at Walchsee in July 1940 with a triangular Nazi-party rally slogan cancel ("Tage der Banner…").

Leaf 04

Germania in Green, Blue and Orange

Four covers, single frankings and a pair of 7½pf oranges.
  • 5pf grey-green Germania

    Cancelled at Hersfeld, addressed to Fräulein Elisabeth Ziegler. A standard single-weight letter rate.

  • 4pf blue-green Hindenburg definitive

    Cancelled at Mainz in 1934, sent to Rauenthal on the Rhine. The engraved portrait die is crisply printed.

  • Pair of 7½pf orange Germania

    Two stamps making up a 15-pfennig rate, addressed to Fräulein Elisabeth Ziegler in Hersfeld. Town cancel reads "…iegand".

  • Pair of 7½pf orange Germania

    A second identical pair on a companion cover to the same Hersfeld address. Consistent with correspondence from the same sender kept together.

Leaf 05

Mourning Covers in Black-Bordered Stationery

Four covers, two on black-bordered "Trauerbrief" mourning stationery.
  • 3pf brown Hindenburg definitive

    On black-bordered mourning stationery to Munich, cancelled at Nürnberg. The border signals a death in the sending household.

  • Weimar-era definitive

    Struck with a purple "Drucksache" (printed matter) handstamp, indicating a reduced printed-matter rate rather than a full letter rate. Addressed to the Ziegler family at Lengsfeld.

  • 3pf olive Reichspost numeral

    An early numeral-design definitive on black-bordered mourning paper, addressed simply to "Herrn Ziegler, Hersfeld". The numeral style dates it to the Reichspost period before Germania's introduction.

  • 10pf red Reichspost Germania

    Cancelled at Dornberg, sent "frei" to Hersfeld. Marked in the corner with the recipient's full family designation.

Leaf 06

Reichspost Carmine, Singly and in Combination

One mixed franking and three single 10pf Reichspost Germanias, all Dornberg-cancelled.
  • 20pf green + 40pf red Germania

    A mixed franking totalling 60 pfennig, likely a registered or foreign letter rate, cancelled at Hersfeld in 1904. Sent to Fräulein Elisabeth Ziegler in Munich.

  • 10pf red Reichspost Germania

    Cancelled at Dornberg, addressed "frei" to Hersfeld. One of several near-identical Dornberg covers on this leaf, suggesting a regular weekly correspondence.

  • 10pf red Reichspost Germania

    Same Dornberg origin and Hersfeld destination as its neighbours. Datestamp shows a year in the 1900s.

  • 10pf red Reichspost Germania

    A third example of the same stamp and route, part of a small run of nearly identical covers. Likely kept together by the original collector as a postmark study.

Leaf 07

Weimar Numerals and a Chemnitz Purple

Five covers including a high-value 60pf Weimar definitive.
  • 10pf red Reichspost stamp

    Addressed to Elis[abeth] Ziegler at Grossalpe. A conventional single-letter franking.

  • 60pf maroon Weimar definitive

    A comparatively high face value, cancelled at Chemnitz on 31.12.22 — the height of the 1922 inflation, when postal rates were rising rapidly. Sent to Pössneck.

  • 3pf brown numeral definitive

    A low-value stamp cancelled at Hersfeld, part of the recurring Ziegler-family correspondence. Franking suits a postcard or printed-matter rate.

  • 3pf brown numeral definitive

    Another Hersfeld-cancelled example, addressed to "Herrn Pfarrer Ziegler". Consistent low-rate domestic use.

  • 15pf green numeral definitive

    Tied by a Hersfeld cancel next to a printed sanatorium advertisement ("Bad Hersfeld Lullusbrunnen") on the envelope itself. The stamp pays a standard letter rate.

Leaf 08

Germania Strips, Pairs, and a Mourning Grey

Four covers to Lehrer P. Ziegler in Hersfeld.
  • 15pf black-grey Germania

    Cancelled at Ziegenhain, addressed by airmail-style routing note "Luftpost" to Hersfeld. A higher-value single franking.

  • Pair of 7½pf orange Germania

    Cancelled at Cassel in April 1918, wartime correspondence to Lehrer (teacher) P. Ziegler. Two stamps make up the 15-pfennig letter rate.

  • Strip of three 5pf grey Germania

    Three stamps totalling 15 pfennig, an unusual way to make up the rate compared with a single higher value. Addressed again to Lehrer P. Ziegler.

  • Grey Germania definitive

    On black-bordered mourning stationery, cancelled in 1906. Sent to the same Ziegler-Hersfeld address as its neighbours on this leaf.

Leaf 09

Reichspost Reds Return, Plus a Mourning Olive

Four covers, two on plain stock and one on black-bordered paper.
  • 10pf red Reichspost Germania

    Cancelled at Dornberg, addressed "frei" to Hersfeld. Matches the run of similar covers seen on Leaf 06.

  • 3pf olive Germania

    On black-bordered mourning stationery, cancelled at what appears to be Freiberg. Addressed to Hersfeld.

  • 10pf red Germania

    Cancelled at Cassel in 1911, addressed to Lehrer P. Ziegler and family. A routine single-rate letter.

  • 5pf green Germania

    Cancelled at Freiberg, sent to Frau Professor Ruschel at Girgehof. Standard low-value domestic postage.

Leaf 10

From Krone/Adler to the Inflation Overprint

Five covers spanning an 1886 lilac issue to a 1920s Berlin overprint.
  • Red overprinted stamp + green Germania

    A combination franking cancelled at Berlin, likely from the inflationary 1920s when values were regularly surcharged. Addressed to Lehrer P. Ziegler, Hersfeld.

  • 3pf brown Germania

    Cancelled at Hersfeld, a straightforward low-value domestic letter. Addressed to "Herrn Lehrer Ziegler und Familie".

  • 3pf lilac pre-Germania definitive

    A small-format eagle/shield design from the Reichspost's Krone/Adler era, cancelled at Cassel in 1886 — the earliest date recorded in this album. Addressed to a building contractor at Dörnberg.

  • 10pf red Reichspost Germania

    Cancelled at Dornberg in 1898, addressed "frei" to Herr Lehrer P. Ziegler in Hersfeld. One of the album's several near-identical Dornberg-Hersfeld covers.

  • 5pf green Germania

    Cancelled at Hersfeld in 1915, wartime domestic mail. Addressed to Fräulein Elisabeth Ziegler, sent "hier" (locally).

Leaf 11

Swastika Slogan Cancels and a Commemorative

Four covers spanning a mourning grey to 1930s Nazi-era cancels.
  • 15pf grey Germania

    On black-bordered mourning stationery, addressed to Fräulein Elisabeth Ziegler in Hersfeld. Standard letter-rate franking.

  • Green Weimar-era definitive

    Cancelled at Frankfurt with a propaganda slogan machine cancel bearing a swastika, dated 1935. Sent to a Dr. jur. at Frankfurt am Main.

  • 3pf "9 November" commemorative

    A brown Nazi-era commemorative issue marking the 1923 Feldherrnhalle putsch anniversary, cancelled at Hersfeld with a swastika slogan strike. Addressed to Rektor a.D. R. Ziegler.

  • 4pf dark green Hindenburg definitive

    Cancelled with a swastika propaganda slogan at a Landgericht (district court) town. Addressed to a wedding couple ("Hochzeitspaar") named Schmitt-Mattes.

Leaf 12

Inflation Hundred-Marks and Two Commemoratives

A 300-mark inflation franking alongside Friedrich and Beethoven commemoratives.
  • Strip of three 100-mark inflation stamps

    A worker/miner design typical of the runaway-inflation issues of 1923, totalling 300 marks and cancelled at Eschwege. Addressed to a "stud. phil." (philosophy student) via Marburg.

  • 10pf red Reichspost Germania

    Cancelled at Cassel, addressed to Herr Dr. jur. Schmidt at Dörnberg. A conventional pre-inflation franking.

  • 15pf green Friedrich der Grosse commemorative

    Cancelled at Fulda in 1927, part of a Weimar-era commemorative set. Addressed within the local Fulda district.

  • 8pf dark green Beethoven commemorative

    Part of the same Weimar commemorative series, sent to Fräulein Käthe Schmitt at Laubenheim. A crisp single-circle cancel confirms origin and date.

Leaf 13

Germania Multiples, One Bound for Bohemia

Four covers, three franked with multiple 5pf Germania stamps.
  • 10pf red Germania

    Cancelled at Dresden, addressed to Frau Olga Reuschel at Sirgehof in Böhmerwald (Bohemian Forest) — the only cross-border destination noted in the album. A single-weight international letter rate.

  • Pair of 5pf green Germania

    A companion cover to the same Böhmerwald address, made up from two 5-pfennig stamps rather than one 10-pfennig value. Cancel again reads Dresden.

  • Pair of 5pf green Germania

    Cancelled at Cassel, addressed to Lehrer P. Ziegler in Hersfeld. Domestic use of the same low-value pair.

  • Strip of three 5pf green Germania

    Three 5-pfennig stamps making up 15 pfennig, addressed to Lehrer P. Ziegler in Hersfeld/Fulda. Illustrates how senders often reached for whatever low-value stamps were on hand.

Leaf 14

Wartime Field Post and the Cassel-Hersfeld Line

Six covers, the oldest bearing an early small-format Reichspost numeral.
  • 5pf grey Germania

    Cancelled at Cassel in 1913, addressed to the "altes Bezirkskommando" (old district military command) in Hersfeld. Pre-war military-administrative mail.

  • 5pf red Germania

    Cancelled at Hersfeld, addressed to Löwenburg near Kassel, marked "frei" for prepaid postage. A modest single franking.

  • 3pf brown Hindenburg definitive

    Cancelled at Hersfeld in 1925, addressed to Rektor i.R. Ziegler at Bad Hersfeld. A retired headmaster's routine correspondence.

  • Pair of 5pf green Germania

    Cancelled at Einhardt, addressed to Fräulein Olly Klinger in Dresden. Two stamps made up the 10-pfennig letter rate.

  • Small early red Reichspost numeral

    A compact numeral-design stamp with a heavily worn cancel, among the oldest-style issues in the album. The cover carries extensive pencilled accounting notes on its reverse.

  • 10pf orange Germania

    Cancelled at Cassel in December 1920, addressed simply "to Herrn Ziegler in Hersfeld über Fulda". Post-war domestic mail on the cusp of the inflation era.

Leaf 15

The Cassel–Hersfeld Circuit, Continued

Five covers, mostly low-value browns and greens between Cassel and Hersfeld.
  • 3pf brown Hindenburg definitive

    On black-bordered mourning stationery, cancelled at Hersfeld in 1934. Addressed simply to "Frau Ziegler, Hersfeld".

  • 5pf green Hindenburg definitive

    Struck with an anti-rheumatism spa advertising slogan for "Bad Hersfeld" mineral springs. A standard single-rate letter.

  • 3pf brown Germania

    Cancelled at Cassel on 31.12.1910 (New Year's Eve), addressed to Lehrer P. Ziegler and family in Hersfeld. Faint accounting figures pencilled above the address.

  • 12pf red Hindenburg definitive

    Cancelled at Cassel-Wilhelmshöhe in 1930, addressed to Dr. Ziegler in Hersfeld. A higher single-value franking than most covers on this leaf.

  • 3pf brown Germania

    Cancelled at Hersfeld in 1910, closing out the leaf's run of small-denomination local mail. Addressed to Herrn Rektor Ziegler and family.

Leaf 16

Dresden's Red Germanias

Four covers to two different recipients, three sharing the same Dresden origin.
  • 10pf red Germania

    Cancelled at Dresden, addressed to Herrn Professor Dr. Karl Reuschel in Wiesbaden. Matches the correspondent seen on Leaf 02.

  • 10pf red Germania

    Also cancelled at Dresden, sent to Fräulein Olga Klinger at a parsonage ("Pfarrhaus") near Ehrenburg. Same denomination and colour as its neighbours on this leaf.

  • 2pf black Germania numeral

    The lowest denomination on this leaf, addressed simply to "Ziegler, Hersfeld". Cancel reads a location ending "-werd".

  • 10pf red Germania

    A third Dresden-cancelled example, addressed to Fräulein Olga Klinger, this time in Hannover. Completes a set of near-identical red Germania frankings that close the album.
