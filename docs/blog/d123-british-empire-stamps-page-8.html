This album was built the old-fashioned way: typed German catalog notes clipped and glued beside the stamps themselves, hand-painted maps marking out each territory, and the occasional postal-history cover tucked in for good measure. It follows the old geography of the British Empire — not modern borders, but the crown colonies, protectorates, condominiums and mandates as they actually issued stamps.

Australia & Oceania

Sheet 71

New South Wales, Tasmania, Victoria & the Commonwealth of Australia

Six colonies federated 1 January 1901

The Dutchman Janszoon reached the Australian mainland in 1605. Ten colonial stamps — Victoria, Tasmania and Queensland — served this transitional period. Tasmania issued its own stamps from 1853–1911, shown here in Mount Wellington and portrait pictorials.

Sheet 72

Queensland, Western Australia & South Australia

Separate colonies before federation

Queensland separated from New South Wales in 1859. Three classic Queen Victoria definitives are shown. No period stamps survive mounted on this page from Western Australia and South Australia, only the historical notes.

Sheet 73

New Zealand

Charted by Tasman 1642 · Dominion of the Commonwealth

Tasman discovered the twin islands in 1642. Stamps issued since 1855 fill two full pages of the album — Victorian Newspaper Postage issues through to a 1966 Christmas Madonna issue.

Sheet 74

Norfolk Island

Within the Australian federation

Eight stamps here include "Founded 1788" landscape pictorials and a series of colourful Christmas commemoratives from 1962–1969.

Sheet 75

Pitcairn Islands

Descendants of the Bounty mutineers · crown colony from 1898

Pitcairn's population descends from the mutinous sailors of HMS Bounty. Seven stamps trace George VI oranges-and-portrait pictorials through Discovery-of-Pitcairn commemoratives.

Sheet 76

Gilbert & Ellice Islands

Split into Kiribati and Tuvalu, 1976–1979

This crown colony became a British protectorate in 1892. Four stamps run from a 1911 protectorate issue through a 1937 Coronation commemorative to a QEII heron pictorial.

Sheet 77

Niue & the Cook Islands

Administered together under New Zealand

Niue became a British protectorate in 1900. The Cook Islands stamps date to 1892, including several island sub-issues shown here with a 1966 South Pacific Games tennis pictorial.

Sheet 78

Tonga, Fiji & Samoa

Three distinct Polynesian island kingdoms

Tonga remained a native kingdom under British protection. Fiji became independent in 1970; six stamps run from an early Edward VII issue through sunbird and orchid pictorials. Samoa split in 1899 into German West Samoa and American East Samoa.

Sheet 79

Melanesia: British Solomon Islands, Nauru & the New Hebrides

Protectorate, mandate territory, and a rare Anglo-French condominium

The British Solomon Islands became a protectorate from 1893/99. Nauru belonged to the German Marshall Islands postal territory until 1914. The New Hebrides were held as a rare Anglo-French condominium from 1906.
