Block Christmas Tree: Where Embroidery Meets Gold Foil

Austrian Post's festive world first pairs traditional guipure lace embroidery with a gleaming gold foil laminate — a Christmas tree you can actually feel

← Back to Philately World
Published by: Philately World Category: Innovative Stamps · Austria 2026 Series: Block Christmas Tree Country: Austria

On 5 November 2026, Austrian Post issued the Block Christmas Tree — a highly unusual philatelic work and a genuine world first. For the first time ever, traditional embroidery has been embellished with a gold foil laminate, bringing together two very different crafts on a single small sheet of philatelic art.

The Block Christmas Tree combines guipure lace embroidery with gold foil lamination — a first in philately.

A Global First in Embroidery and Foil

Austria has a long and celebrated tradition of textile stamps, and this issue takes that heritage a step further. The design depicts an embroidered green Christmas tree, richly decorated and finished with the word "Austria". What makes it extraordinary is the marriage of materials: the soft, tactile texture of guipure lace embroidery sits alongside reflective gold foil, creating a shimmering contrast that changes as the stamp catches the light.

The result is a stamp that appeals to the senses — the raised threads invite touch, while the gold foil adds a festive sparkle that photography can only partly capture. It is a fitting tribute to the Christmas season and a remarkable feat of small-format craftsmanship.

A world first: never before has traditional embroidery been combined with a gold foil laminate on a postage stamp.

Crafted by Hämmerle & Vogel

The Block Christmas Tree was both designed and printed by Hämmerle & Vogel GmbH & Co KG, the Austrian embroidery company behind several of Austria's most admired textile stamps. Their expertise in fine needlework is evident in the precision of the stitching and the delicate rendering of the tree's branches and decorations.

The print style — guipure lace embroidery plus gold foil lamination — represents a technical breakthrough. Guipure lace is characterised by its open, interconnected motifs without a background net, and applying it together with metallic foil demands exceptional control during production.

Collector Appeal

Embroidery stamps have always been among the most popular modern issues, and a gold foil first raises the bar considerably. The Block Christmas Tree sits at the crossroads of several collecting themes: Christmas philately, textile and embroidery stamps, innovative printing techniques, and Austrian modern issues.

With a print run of just over 101,710 copies, it is also comparatively limited for a seasonal issue. Collectors who appreciate tactile, unusual formats will want to secure one early — and a Christmas theme gives it year-round display appeal.

Why It Matters

The Block Christmas Tree is more than a seasonal greeting in miniature. It marks a new chapter in the long history of Austrian embroidery stamps, proving that traditional craft and modern material innovation can still produce firsts in philately. For anyone building a collection around unusual stamps or textile philately, this festive issue is a standout piece.
